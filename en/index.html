Seven points worth highlighting

1

Volume holds the step-up

After the impressive jump in Q2, network volume held at $3.6B in Q3, an annualized run-rate of about $14B. The company keeps delivering and executing on its plans even as market conditions shift. This is a crucial point: we can see the company meeting its commitments even when the wind turns and blows against it.

Klarna, which has left the network, needs to be stripped out. Without it, the quarter's volume was about $3.3B, and that is the real base for measuring Q4. On the other hand, the two new banks, Citizens and FNBO, only joined this quarter. A new partner takes about a year to reach full volume, so most of their contribution is still ahead. The company also reported growing one partner's originations by 300% by embedding additional products. Rolling out the full product suite across all partners, together with a partner pipeline that has grown 50%, makes further significant growth in loan volume possible.

Higher rates do make life harder for the company, but I expect them to push large banks to join the network over the next two years: banks that want to raise their return on equity without growing their balance sheet, and banks that want to keep their customers close without taking on the credit risk. Notably, management is already talking about megabanks in the pipeline. That could enable another step-up in origination volume. There is another advantage: every new partner increases the data the company sees, allowing it to sharpen its models and offer better products.

What the data says
  • Network volume: $2.624B in Q1, $3.535B in Q2 (a 35% jump), $3.605B in Q3: +2% QoQ and +29% YoY.
  • Q3 annualized run-rate of $14.4B, 37% above all of 2025 ($10.5B). 2026 guidance: +21%–24%.
  • Klarna contributed about $330M in Q3. According to the CFO, it was not material to FRLPC or profit. Q4 is the first full quarter without it.
  • Implied Q4 guidance: $2.961–3.261B. Excluding Klarna, the seasonal decline is expected to be 5%–6%, versus 9% in Q4 2025.
  • Application volume: $377B (+42%), with a conversion rate of about 1%.
  • Six new partners so far this year, against an annual target of 2–4. The pipeline is more than 50% larger than a year ago and, according to Krubiner, includes megabanks.
  • Krubiner: a mature bank partner can contribute tens of millions of dollars a year in contribution margin.
Sources: Q3 shareholder letter, Q3 earnings supplement, Oct 29 earnings call, 2025 annual report
2

FRLPC touches the floor

The FRLPC rate keeps falling and now stands at 4.1%, very close to the bottom of the target range set by Gal Krubiner, 4%. The decline comes mainly from higher rates, which eat into fees, and from onboarding six new partners this year, since past experience shows a new partner starts out less profitable and improves over time.

The company raised loan rates to offset the rate increases that significantly eroded profitability, and expects to return in 2027 to around the middle of the range, about 4.5%. Such a move could lift net income sharply. I'll be watching this number closely in the coming reports: can the company raise rates that are already high while keeping borrower quality intact?

Counterintuitively, the company says the increase didn't reduce activation. Perhaps that's because rates are already so high that another one or two percentage points don't change much for the borrower.

$200M in annualized earnings

Net income is stabilizing too, at an annualized run-rate of about $200M, thanks to the jump in volume. It shows that profitability is no longer a fluke: this is the pace we can expect the company to sustain and even increase.

At the $19.23 close, the adjusted P/E on the latest quarter's run-rate is under 5 (about 9 on a GAAP basis). That is a very low multiple for a growing company like Pagaya, and one of the main reasons I believe there is significant upside here, especially when you look at peer multiples.

4

Funding costs, and a balance sheet that lowers them

Higher rates are raising the company's funding costs. That is a significant burden, and I expect it to reduce activity in the coming quarters until the trend turns.

2-year US Treasury yield

Monthly average from October 2025 to September 2026; the last point is the Oct 29, 2026 close. The yield rose from 3.47% in February to 4.61%, about 115 basis points, with about 43 basis points of that in September alone. This is the base rate Pagaya's loans and securitizations are priced off.

On the other hand, spreads on the company's latest securitizations tightened, even as, according to TD Cowen analyst Moshe Orenbuch, industry spreads widened toward the end of the quarter. In my view the company will face a real challenge raising another forward flow facility while the corporate bond market is pricing rates this high. Perhaps for that reason, the company keeps diversifying its funding sources and will soon start funding part of its originations through a facility from a large bank, which should be cheaper than existing sources. We'll keep a close eye on the company's announcements about new funding.

Also worth noting is the company's growing balance sheet, which is becoming collateral that can be turned into lower rates on its leverage. For example, the cost of secured borrowings fell to 8.5%, from 11.5% in mid-2025. It is also a structure that serves shareholders: bonds yielding about 11%, funded in the latest deal at about 6.5%, non-recourse to the company.

What the data says
  • On the latest PAID deal (personal loans), spreads tightened by more than 30bp on the investment-grade tranches and more than 20bp overall.
  • The company raised $3B of ABS in the quarter: $1.8B in personal loans and $1.2B in auto. The investor base grew from about 170 to more than 180.
  • Funding mix: a year ago about 75% came from prefunded ABS; today about 50%.
  • More than $2B of committed capital: an auto forward flow with Neuberger Berman ($700M), a first personal loan VFN (about $700M), and a second revolving securitization supporting $850M. Another bank-led warehouse is expected in Q4.
  • Guidance assumes the cost of capital stays at current levels, which are higher than last quarter. Sanjiv Das: the company is willing to accept a temporary slowdown in volume growth to protect unit economics.
  • Investments in loans and securities: $1,100.8M, versus $1,040.1M in June, $945.3M in December and $887.7M a year ago. Notes make up 49% of the portfolio. As a share of LTM volume, the portfolio is stable at around 9%.
  • Secured borrowings (non-recourse): $314.3M, versus $253.0M in June and $131.5M a year ago. CFO: the latest deal with a major bank is funded at about 6.5%, and the average cost is expected to keep falling.
  • The portfolio generates interest income: $29.0M in the quarter (+30% QoQ, +94% YoY). Annualized, about $116M, versus $48.4M for all of 2025.
  • Attributable equity: $622.4M, versus $480.0M in December. According to the company, up about 50% since the end of 2024.
  • My calculation: corporate debt ($648M) less unrestricted cash ($247M) gives net debt of about $400M, about 0.9x LTM adjusted EBITDA (about $446M).
Sources: Q3 shareholder letter, Q3 earnings supplement, quarterly balance sheets, Oct 29 earnings call, 2025 annual report

6

Higher-quality borrowers

The shift in the partner mix is bringing in higher-quality borrowers. The average personal loan borrower now earns $120K a year. In auto loans, average income rose from $62K to $88K, and collateral quality improved: average vehicle mileage fell from 65K to 24K miles. According to the company, the average FICO score is in the 680–700 range.

Beyond the borrower profile, actual credit performance strengthens the picture. 2025 vintages show cumulative losses about 40% lower than the 2022 vintage in auto loans, and about two-thirds lower than the peak late-2021 vintage in personal loans. Rating agency KBRA upgraded 63 securitization tranches from 2025 vintages with no downgrades, and the company left its expected loss assumption for the portfolio unchanged. That said, keep in mind that the benchmark vintages were especially weak, and the 2026 vintages are still too young to judge.

Write-downs on loan holdings

In the last quarter the company wrote down $77M of the value of its holdings due to impairment, more than 7% of total holdings at the start of the quarter. That is far from a negligible write-down rate, but it is part of the business model: the company retains risk in its own securitizations. And even after the write-downs, it is posting record profits.

The number is worth breaking down. Only about $43M went through the income statement, and that level has been stable for four quarters while volume grew. The rest, about $32M, went through other comprehensive income directly to equity. It stems mainly from a higher discount rate, driven by drastic changes in the external rate environment, and not from the credit loss assumption, which didn't change. We'll keep watching this figure closely to make sure the company's credit quality holds up.

Bottom line

Mainly because of the global rate environment, I don't expect the stock to rise over the next six months. Q4 has traditionally been seasonally weak, and the rate environment makes it harder for the company to grow volume.

That said, I believe the stock is significantly undervalued, because Pagaya proved this quarter that it can make money even when rates work against it. Volume is holding, costs are frozen, funding is diversifying and getting cheaper, and borrower quality is improving. The pressure on FRLPC and funding costs is real but, according to management, temporary, and the current price treats it as permanent. I believe the stock has significant upside as long as management keeps delivering on the business plans it laid out on the earnings call, mainly the return of FRLPC to 4.5% and continued onboarding of new partners, with an emphasis on megabanks.

What I'll be watching in the coming quarters:

  • The FRLPC rate: whether it starts rising toward the end of Q4 and into Q1 2027, as promised.
  • Q4 volume against the $2.96–3.26B range, and a base of $3.3B excluding Klarna.
  • Announcements of more large banks, above all a first megabank.
  • Impairments as a share of volume, adjustments through other comprehensive income, and the effective tax rate.
